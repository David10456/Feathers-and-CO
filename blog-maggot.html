Maggot Rearing for Poultry Feed: Sustainable Protein Innovation

  • Feathers and CO Team
  • Aug 12, 2026
  • 4 Comments

At Feathers and CO POULTRY FARM, innovation is at the heart of everything we do. One of our most groundbreaking practices is the rearing of maggots—specifically Black Soldier Fly (BSF) larvae—as a high-protein supplement for our poultry feed. This sustainable approach not only reduces our dependence on expensive commercial protein sources like fishmeal but also helps us recycle organic waste from our farm and local community into valuable nutrition for our birds.

Black Soldier Fly larvae are nutritional powerhouses, containing between 40-45% crude protein and essential amino acids like lysine and methionine, which are critical for poultry growth and egg production. By integrating maggot meal into our feed formulation, we have been able to replace up to 25% of traditional protein sources while maintaining, and even improving, the health and productivity of our 1,000 broilers, 20 agric-fowls, and 30 old layers.

"Maggot rearing transforms what would be waste into gold. It's a circular economy solution that cuts our feed costs by nearly 30% while producing healthier, more nutritious chicken and eggs for our customers."

— Feathers and CO Production Manager

Our maggot rearing process begins with the collection of organic waste materials, including fruit and vegetable scraps from local markets, brewery waste, and properly composted poultry manure. These materials are placed in controlled breeding units where adult Black Soldier Flies lay their eggs. Within 24-48 hours, the eggs hatch into tiny larvae that begin feeding voraciously on the organic substrate.

The Rearing Process: From Waste to Protein

The larvae are reared in specially designed containers with proper ventilation and temperature control, maintained between 27-30°C for optimal growth. Over a period of 14-18 days, the larvae grow from microscopic hatchlings to mature prepupae, increasing their biomass by up to 5,000 times. During this phase, they consume nearly twice their body weight in organic waste daily, making them incredibly efficient bioconverters.

Once the larvae reach the prepupal stage, indicated by their dark coloration and reduced feeding activity, they are harvested using automated sieving systems. The harvested larvae are then rinsed thoroughly and subjected to a 24-hour fasting period to clear their digestive tracts, ensuring the final product is clean and safe for poultry consumption.

Processing and Quality Control

After the fasting period, the larvae are either used fresh or processed into maggot meal for longer storage. Our processing method involves blanching the larvae in hot water at 90°C for 5 minutes to kill any potential pathogens, followed by sun-drying or mechanical drying until the moisture content drops below 10%. The dried larvae are then milled into a fine, protein-rich powder that can be easily incorporated into our feed mixtures.

Every batch of maggot meal undergoes rigorous quality testing. We check for protein content, moisture levels, and the absence of harmful bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli. This commitment to quality ensures that our poultry receive only the safest, most nutritious feed possible.

Nutritional Benefits for Poultry

The inclusion of maggot meal in poultry feed offers numerous benefits. First, the high protein content supports rapid muscle development in broilers, helping them reach market weight 5-7 days faster than birds fed conventional diets. For our layers, the balanced amino acid profile results in larger eggs with stronger shells and richer, more vibrant yolks.

Additionally, BSF larvae contain natural antimicrobial peptides and lauric acid, which have been shown to boost the immune system of poultry and reduce the incidence of common diseases. Since we started incorporating maggot meal into our feed regimen, we have observed a noticeable improvement in the overall vitality of our flock and a reduction in veterinary costs.

Environmental and Economic Impact

Beyond the direct benefits to our poultry, maggot rearing aligns perfectly with our sustainability mission. Each week, our maggot production units process hundreds of kilograms of organic waste that would otherwise end up in landfills or contribute to environmental pollution. In return, we produce high-quality protein meal, creating a closed-loop system that minimizes waste and maximizes resource efficiency.

Economically, this innovation has been transformative. By producing our own protein supplement, we have significantly reduced our monthly feed costs, allowing us to offer our customers more competitive prices without compromising on quality. This cost savings, combined with the improved health and productivity of our birds, has enhanced the overall profitability and sustainability of Feathers and CO.
