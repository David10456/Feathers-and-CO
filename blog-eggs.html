Egg Production and Collection Management: From Nest to Table with Care

  • Feathers and CO Team
  • Aug 14, 2026
  • 3 Comments

Eggs are one of the most nutritious, versatile, and widely consumed foods in Cameroon. At Feathers and CO POULTRY FARM, our egg production program is a cornerstone of our business, serving households, restaurants, bakeries, and retailers across Yaoundé. While our 30 old layers may seem modest compared to industrial operations, every single egg they produce receives the same meticulous attention to quality, safety, and freshness that defines our entire brand.

Producing high-quality eggs is not simply a matter of keeping hens and collecting what they lay. It requires a deep understanding of layer nutrition, housing design, daily management routines, careful handling, and proper storage. At Feathers and CO, we have developed a comprehensive egg production and collection management system that ensures every egg reaching our customers is fresh, clean, nutritious, and safe.

"An egg is a complete food, but its quality is only as good as the care given to the hen that laid it. We treat every egg as if it were going to our own family's table—because often, it is."

— Feathers and CO Layer House Manager

Our Layer Flock: The Foundation of Quality Eggs

Our egg production comes from a carefully selected flock of 30 old layers—hardy, dual-purpose birds well-suited to the Cameroonian climate. We specifically chose older layer breeds for several reasons:

  • Climate Resilience: Old layers are more heat-tolerant and disease-resistant than exotic commercial hybrids, making them ideal for our tropical environment without excessive reliance on climate control.
  • Rich Yolks: Their diverse diet (including our home-grown maize, soybeans, and maggot meal) produces eggs with noticeably darker, richer yolks that customers consistently praise.
  • Longevity: These birds have longer productive lifespans, providing steady egg production over multiple years with proper care.
  • Lower Stress: Older breeds are generally calmer and less prone to stress-related production drops, resulting in more consistent output.

Our layers are housed in a dedicated section of our poultry facility, separate from the broilers, with comfortable nest boxes, perches, and deep litter flooring. The house is designed to maximize natural light while providing adequate ventilation and protection from predators.

Daily Collection Procedures: Timing and Technique Matter

Egg collection is one of the most critical daily tasks on our farm. How, when, and how carefully eggs are collected directly impacts their quality and shelf life. Our collection protocol includes:

  • Collection Frequency: Eggs are collected a minimum of three times daily—at 8:00 AM, 12:00 PM, and 4:00 PM. This frequent collection prevents eggs from sitting in nests for extended periods, reducing the risk of cracking, contamination, or embryo development (in fertilized eggs).
  • Gentle Handling: Staff are trained to handle eggs with extreme care, placing them gently into clean, padded collection trays. Rough handling causes microscopic cracks in shells that allow bacteria to enter, dramatically reducing shelf life.
  • Clean vs. Dirty Separation: Eggs are immediately sorted into categories: clean (Grade A), slightly dirty (Grade B), and heavily soiled (rejected for sale, used internally or composted). This ensures only the highest quality eggs reach customers.
  • Documentation: Every collection is recorded in our farm management system, noting the number of eggs, time, house section, and any quality observations. This data helps us track production patterns and identify issues early.
  • Hygiene Protocol: Staff wash hands thoroughly before collection and wear clean gloves. Collection trays are sanitized daily. Eggs are never washed with water (which can remove the protective bloom and allow bacteria to penetrate the shell) unless absolutely necessary, and then only with sanitized warm water and immediate drying.

Egg Grading and Quality Control

Not all eggs are created equal. Our rigorous grading system ensures customers receive only the best:

  • Visual Inspection: Every egg is examined for shell integrity, cleanliness, shape, and color. Cracked, misshapen, or heavily soiled eggs are removed from the sale stream.
  • Size Categorization: Eggs are sorted by weight into standard categories: Small (under 45g), Medium (45-55g), Large (55-65g), and Extra-Large (over 65g). This ensures consistent sizing within each carton.
  • Candling (Periodic): A sample from each batch is candled (examined with a bright light) to check for internal defects like blood spots, meat spots, or thin shells. This quality control step catches issues that visual inspection cannot detect.
  • Shell Quality Assessment: Shell thickness and strength are regularly tested. Thin or porous shells are indicators of nutritional deficiencies or health issues in the flock, triggering immediate investigation.
  • Rejection Criteria: Any egg with cracks, leaks, abnormal odor, or visible contamination is rejected from sale. We would rather lose revenue than compromise customer trust or food safety.

Storage and Handling: Preserving Freshness

Proper storage is essential for maintaining egg quality from collection to customer. Our storage protocols include:

  • Temperature Control: Eggs are stored in a dedicated cool room maintained at 15-18°C with 70-80% relative humidity. This temperature slows bacterial growth and preserves egg quality without freezing.
  • Proper Positioning: Eggs are stored with the pointed end down, which keeps the yolk centered and the air cell at the blunt end, preserving freshness and internal quality.
  • FIFO System: We strictly follow First-In, First-Out inventory management. Older eggs are sold first, ensuring customers always receive the freshest possible product.
  • Shelf Life Management: Eggs are dated at collection and have a maximum shelf life of 28 days from laying under proper storage conditions. We typically sell eggs within 7-10 days of collection for optimal freshness.
  • Avoiding Temperature Fluctuations: Eggs are never moved between extreme temperatures (e.g., from cold storage to hot outdoor conditions) as this causes condensation on shells, which can facilitate bacterial penetration.
  • Protection from Strong Odors: Eggs are stored away from strong-smelling substances (onions, fish, chemicals) as their porous shells can absorb odors, affecting taste.

Layer Nutrition: Feeding for Optimal Production

The quality and quantity of eggs produced are directly tied to layer nutrition. Our specialized layer feeding program includes:

  • Balanced Layer Mash: Our custom layer feed contains 17% crude protein, 4% calcium, and all essential amino acids, vitamins, and minerals needed for consistent egg production and strong shells.
  • Calcium Supplementation: Free-choice oyster shell grit is provided in separate feeders, allowing hens to self-regulate calcium intake based on their individual needs, especially important for older layers producing larger eggs.
  • Water Quality: Clean, fresh water is available 24/7 through nipple drinkers. Water consumption directly impacts egg production—layers that are even slightly dehydrated will reduce or stop laying.
  • Lighting Program: Our layers receive 16 hours of light per day (natural + supplemental LED), which stimulates consistent egg production. Sudden changes in lighting duration can cause production drops or double-yolk eggs.
  • Maggot Meal Inclusion: Our innovative maggot meal supplement provides natural carotenoids that contribute to the rich, orange yolk color our customers love, along with essential amino acids for egg quality.
  • Stress Reduction: We minimize stressors (noise, predators, sudden temperature changes) as stress hormones directly suppress egg production.

Common Egg Quality Issues and Our Solutions

Even with excellent management, egg quality issues can arise. Here is how we address the most common ones:

  • Thin or Soft Shells: Usually indicates calcium deficiency or vitamin D3 imbalance. Solution: Increase calcium supplementation, verify feed formulation, ensure adequate sunlight exposure for natural vitamin D synthesis.
  • Blood Spots: Caused by small blood vessel rupture during egg formation. Not a safety concern but affects appearance. Solution: Ensure adequate vitamin K in diet, minimize stress during laying.
  • Meat Spots: Small tissue fragments in the egg white, usually from oviduct irritation. Solution: Check for oviduct infections, ensure proper nutrition, reduce stress.
  • Double Yolks: Two yolks in one egg, common in young layers or after lighting changes. Not a defect but affects sizing. Solution: Maintain consistent lighting program, allow young hens to mature gradually.
  • Dirty Eggs: Caused by dirty nest boxes or wet litter. Solution: Frequent litter replacement, clean nest boxes daily, ensure proper drainage to keep litter dry.
  • Reduced Production: Can be caused by stress, disease, nutritional deficiency, or molting. Solution: Systematic investigation of environment, health, nutrition, and lighting; address root cause.

Packaging and Distribution: The Final Step

Proper packaging protects eggs during transport and communicates quality to customers:

  • Carton Options: We offer eggs in 6-egg, 15-egg, and 30-egg cartons to suit different customer needs, from individual households to restaurants and bakeries.
  • Protective Packaging: All cartons are made of molded pulp or recycled cardboard that cushions eggs and prevents breakage during handling and transport.
  • QR Code Labeling: Every carton features a unique QR code that customers can scan to see the collection date, feed composition of the laying hens, health certifications, and farm location—complete transparency from nest to table.
  • Date Stamping: Each carton is clearly stamped with the collection date and best-before date (28 days from collection), allowing customers to verify freshness.
  • Delivery Care: Our delivery team is trained to handle egg cartons with care, avoiding stacking heavy items on top and protecting them from temperature extremes during transport.
  • Customer Education: We provide storage tips on our packaging and website, helping customers maintain egg quality after purchase.

Economic Impact of Our Egg Production

While our 30 layers may seem small, they contribute significantly to our business:

  • Steady Daily Revenue: With an average production rate of 70-80% (21-24 eggs daily), our layers generate consistent daily income that helps smooth cash flow between broiler batch sales.
  • High Customer Loyalty: Egg customers tend to be very loyal, purchasing weekly or subscribing to regular deliveries, providing predictable recurring revenue.
  • Premium Pricing Justification: Our commitment to quality—rich yolks, clean shells, QR traceability—allows us to command premium prices compared to generic market eggs.
  • Gateway Product: Many customers first discover Feathers and CO through our eggs, then expand to purchasing chicken, manure, or attending our training programs.
  • Low Input Cost: Once layers are established, daily feed and care costs are relatively low compared to the revenue generated, making egg production highly profitable.

Results: The Feathers and CO Egg Difference

Our comprehensive egg production and collection management system delivers measurable results:

  • Consistent Production: We maintain an average laying rate of 75-80%, well above the 60-65% average for small farms in Cameroon.
  • Low Breakage Rate: Our careful handling and packaging result in less than 2% breakage, compared to industry averages of 5-8%.
  • High Customer Satisfaction: Our eggs consistently receive praise for yolk color, freshness, and taste, with many customers noting the visible difference compared to other brands.
  • Zero Food Safety Incidents: Our strict hygiene and quality control protocols have resulted in zero customer complaints about egg safety or quality issues.
  • Premium Market Position: Our eggs are sought after by high-end restaurants, health-conscious families, and bakeries who value quality and traceability.

At Feathers and CO, egg production is not just about quantity—it is about delivering a product that families trust to nourish their children, that chefs rely on for their reputation, and that represents the very best of Cameroonian agriculture. From the careful nutrition of our layers to the gentle collection, rigorous grading, proper storage, and transparent packaging, every step is designed to ensure that when you crack open a Feathers and CO egg, you experience the difference that care, expertise, and commitment to quality make. This is our promise, delivered fresh to your table every single day.

I have been buying your eggs for months and I love them! But I always wonder—how can I tell at home if an egg is still fresh after I buy it? I have heard about the water test but I am not sure if it really works. Also, should I store eggs in the fridge or at room temperature here in Yaoundé?

Feathers and CO Team Reply
Aug 15, 2026

Hello Marie-Claire! Thank you for being such a loyal customer! Great questions. Yes, the water test absolutely works—here is how: fill a glass with cold water and gently place the egg in it. A very fresh egg will sink and lie flat on its side. An egg that is 1-2 weeks old will sink but stand upright on its blunt end. An egg that floats is old and should be discarded (the air cell inside has expanded as the egg aged). For storage in Yaoundé's warm climate, we strongly recommend refrigeration at home. While eggs can be kept at room temperature for short periods, refrigeration at 4-8°C dramatically extends freshness and safety. Just keep them in their carton with the pointed end down, and away from the fridge door where temperature fluctuates. Thank you for trusting us with your family's nutrition! 🙏

Feathers and CO Team Reply

Hello Paul! Congratulations on starting your layer farm—50 birds is a great start! The most common mistakes that reduce egg production in small farms are: 1) Inconsistent lighting (layers need 16 hours of light daily—sudden changes or insufficient light dramatically reduce production); 2) Inadequate calcium (thin shells and reduced production often trace back to calcium deficiency); 3) Dirty or wet litter (causes dirty eggs and stress); 4) Irregular feeding times (layers thrive on routine); 5) Stress from predators, noise, or overcrowding. If I had to pick the single most important focus, it would be NUTRITION—specifically a properly balanced layer feed with adequate calcium and protein. Many small farmers try to save money by feeding kitchen scraps or unbalanced mixtures, which directly impacts production. Invest in quality layer feed first, then optimize other factors. We actually have a dedicated Layer Management module in our Farmer Training Workshops where we cover all of this in detail with hands-on practice.
